# Tessera compared with online word games

*Written 28 October 2026. Prices and features checked against public pages on that date.*

**Short version:** Tessera is not really competing with Woogles, ISC, Scrabble GO or
UniWords. They are online services with player pools; Tessera is a Windows desktop game
with strong bots and analysis. Tessera wins on offline play, tournament word lists, bot
strength and after-game analysis. It loses on everything social: no accounts, no
matchmaking, no chat, no ratings ladder, no phone or browser version.

| | Price | Where | Play style | Standout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tessera** | Free (this project) | Windows desktop | Offline bots, 2-4 players hosted by a friend on the LAN, or a tournament run by one of them | Quackle bots, CSW24/SOWPODS, move-by-move analysis, house rules, spectators, three tournament formats |
| **Woogles** | Free, donation-funded non-profit | Browser | Live rated games, tournaments, puzzles | Serious tournament play, open source, both word lists, strong bots, chat |
| **ISC** | Free; paid membership for extras | Browser (was Wordbiz) | Live games, seeks, kibitzing | The old home of tournament Scrabble; many languages, ~35 bots, deep ratings |
| **Scrabble GO** | Free with ads; gems; Club about $5-8/month | iOS/Android | Async games, leagues, boosters | Official licence, 45M installs, very polished, very monetised |
| **UniWords** | Free, no ads, no purchases | iOS/Android/web | Async, tournaments, ELO | Clean indie alternative; English + Danish, Oxford dictionary, word explanations |

## Where Tessera wins

- **No internet needed.** Bots, rules and analysis all run on the machine.
- **Bots that actually play.** Quackle at Club/Expert/Master is stronger than the
  built-in opponents in Scrabble GO or UniWords.
- **Real tournament lexicons.** CSW24 and SOWPODS, hash-verified, not an app's own
  house dictionary.
- **Analysis.** Best play per turn, equity lost, verdicts and a summary. The free apps
  either do not offer this or charge for it.
- **Self-hosted games.** Two to four people plus spectators, hosted by one player,
  with house rules the host controls.
- **Self-hosted tournaments.** Swiss, round robin or knockout, filled with friends or
  bots, one live table at a time and a ladder between matches. Woogles has real
  tournaments with many entrants; the other apps do not let a player run one.

## Where Tessera loses

- No strangers to play: no accounts, no lobby, no matchmaking, no ratings.
- No chat. (A dropped player does get a minute to reconnect, three times per
  game, before forfeiting.)
- Windows only, needs Python and an install; no phone or browser build.
- LAN-style networking only; exposing it to the open internet needs a token check first.

## The honest positioning

Tessera is closer to a training and club tool than a Scrabble GO rival. Against Woogles
it is the offline equivalent: same serious approach, no community. Against Scrabble GO
and UniWords it is the opposite trade: better play and analysis, far less convenience.

If Tessera is to compete online, the missing pieces in rough order are accounts, a
lobby with matchmaking, chat, a rating system, and then a web or mobile client. The
game itself is already ahead of most of them, and it now survives the dropped
connections that plague casual online games.
